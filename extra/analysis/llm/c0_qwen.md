## Reading and decisions

- **The decline test does not fire on noise.** Comparing the base model with itself gives 0
  declines at FDR 10% in either direction, over 3,000 problems.
- **But at N = 32 it can't see the losses that matter.** A problem can only be flagged if it falls
  to 0 from p̂ ≥ 0.375 (BH needs p ≈ 1e-4).
  - In the lr 1e-6 run, 15 problems went from [0.05, 0.2) to below 0.05, and none was flagged.
  - The toy places absorption at low p, so **the planned N = 256 second stage is required** for C1.
    At N = 256 the threshold falls to 0.055.
  - Cheaper alternative for "before": pool the two base evaluations (64 samples, threshold 0.20).
- **Learning rate for C1: 1e-6.** That is the pre-registered rule: the smallest lr at which mean p̂
  moves.
  - Mean p̂ rises from 0.41 to 0.75 in 150 steps.
  - No declines, no losses, 783 significant gains.
  - 3e-6 looks much the same: 0.80, with 2 declines and 1 loss, about what FDR noise allows.
- **lr 1e-5 is a collapse, not selective loss.**
  - Training reward falls from the first block (0.52 → 0.34), and mean p̂ ends below base (0.36).
  - 247 problems decline and 168 are lost, spread over every base bin. Even 15 problems that
    started at p̂ ≥ 0.8 are lost.
  - Format failures stay near 0, so this is not a parsing artefact.
  - This is the C5 "lr × 10" arm already observed. It shows that a learning rate that is too large
    produces mass losses, but not the minority mechanism.
- **Problem pool:** 2,239 of 3,000 train problems have pooled base p̂ in [0.1, 0.9]. C1 trains and
  evaluates on these ids (`extra/llm/pools/qwen.json`).
- **Train against test:** base p̂ is 0.42 on train and 0.32 on test. C1 reports them separately,
  as planned.
- **Throughput:** 13–15 s per step at 64 prompts × 8. So 300 steps take about 1.2 h, and 500 steps
  about 2 h, per run.
